# Opening a US bank account without an SSN or US credit history

Most US banks and credit unions — including school-partnered ones — allow
international students to open a basic checking account using:
- Passport
- I-20 (F-1) or DS-2019 (J-1)
- Proof of local address (often a signed housing/dorm agreement is accepted
  before a permanent lease exists)

An SSN or ITIN is **usually not required** to open the account itself, though
some banks may ask for one later to enable certain features (e.g. some credit
products). If a bank representative says an SSN is mandatory just to open a
basic checking account, that's worth double-checking against the specific
bank's international-student policy — it's a common point of confusion, and
policies do vary by institution.

Building US credit history (relevant for future apartment leases, car loans,
etc.) with no prior credit file typically starts with:
- A **secured credit card** (deposit-backed, easiest to qualify for with no
  credit history), or
- Becoming an **authorized user** on a family member's or trusted friend's
  existing card, if available.
