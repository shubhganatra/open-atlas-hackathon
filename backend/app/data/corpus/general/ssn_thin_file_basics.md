# How SSN applications generally work for thin-file international students

An SSN is only strictly required for on-campus payroll (to get *paid*
correctly) — it is generally **not** required to open a checking account, get
a mobile phone plan, or sign most leases, contrary to a common assumption.

General sequence, independent of school:
1. Have a qualifying job offer (on-campus, or off-campus authorization like
   CPT/OPT).
2. Your school's international office issues documentation certifying you're
   eligible to apply (exact mechanics vary by school — see the
   university-specific bucket for the precise process).
3. Visit an SSA office in person with that documentation plus immigration
   paperwork (I-20/DS-2019, passport, visa, I-94).
4. Processing after the SSA visit typically takes **2–4 weeks** for the
   physical card to arrive by mail.
5. You can usually **start working before the SSN arrives** — most employers
   can onboard you with an "SSN applied for" status and correct payroll
   records once it's issued.

This is why the sequencing matters: a student with no job offer is blocked
much earlier in the chain (they can't get the eligibility documentation at
all) than a student who has a job offer but hasn't yet completed paperwork —
even though both currently "don't have an SSN."
